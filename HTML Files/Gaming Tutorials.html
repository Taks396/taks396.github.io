Gaming Tutorials

By Taks

Description

This page serves to show off the different things I have done in the different video games I play. It won't contain anything that can't be explained simply, like Call of Duty, where the objective is just get in and shoot stuff, but instead will contain games that require some explanation, like how I build something in that game for example.

7 Days to Die

This section is dedicated to one of my favorite video games of all time, 7 Days to Die. This is an open world apocalypse survival sandbox that I played religiously through high school and college. Due to it being a sandbox, there are many playstyles that are viable and frankly the game spells them out for you via the leveling system. What this page will be for then is how to create the different builds I have made for this game and what versions they were built in. Pictures for all of these are provided so you can build along with me.

The Blender

This is my go to base design ever since I started playing 7 Days to Die on the PC. It is inspired by a base created by Skippy(wtf was his username?) during an Alpha 19 playthrough he had running at the time. I thought the base was cool, but very unoptimized for what it was doing. So, in my own Alpha 19 playthrough, I recreated and then slowly refined this design over the next couple Alphas. Forewarning however, ever since the inclusion of the Learn by Reading system, this design struggles heavily in the early game.

GregTech: New Horizons

This section is dedicated to the process I have generally followed when attempting to beat GregTech: New Horizons (GTNH), a notoriously long and challenging Minecraft mod-pack. The pack adds a ton of features that are in the base game, so a lot of things might look normal, but could easily behave differently than vanilla. I will go through each of the stages in the quest book sequentially, but I will be referring to content at the bottom of this page where appropriate. In general, the quest book walks you through everything that you "need" to do, but I find there are a lot of side quests that are so useful they really should be mandatory. Before we get started, you might want to refer to the tips section for setup advice or things I have learned that don't fit in any one quest tier.

For each quest tier, you may notice a lot of bolded words that do not get bolded again later on. If you hover over these, you will see the crafting recipe to make that item. I do this for your convenience because trying to keep track of the thousands of crafting recipes needed to complete this pack is a nightmare. GTNH has this functionality built in with the Never Enough Items (NEI) mod, but I feel like giving you the recipe when it's first introduced is helpful. The only time I will re-bold an item is when the crafting recipe changes, like when we start crafting items with the assembler in the LV Tier.

Finally, at the bottom of every quest tier, I will give you a list of the minimum number of raw materials you will need to complete that tiers' main quest line. You may find this unhelpful because there are tons of different ways to complete the tiers with no one-size-fits-all solution, but the point is to give you a rough idea of what you'll need to get for that tier. There are some recipes that are more resource efficient but less time efficient and vice versa, so use this resource list as a general guide, not an absolute must.

And So It Begins

You have just spawned in and if you are new, you probably have no idea what you are doing. As tedious as GTNH can be, it will at least guide you through everything you need to do via that quest book you're holding. For this first quest tier, there isn't that much I can talk about that isn't spelled out in the quest book, but I'm still going to walk you through the main tasks anyway. Your biggest concern right now is the fast approaching night which starts at 6:32pm. One of the 310 installed mods in this pack makes the night completely pitch black. A separate mod spawns a bunch of different and more dangerous versions of the vanilla mobs. Your first task: dig a hole to hunker down in for the time being. You need to gather dirt for the first quest anyway.

Next on the agenda: crafting and tools. One of the many joys of GTNH is that the crafting chain for everything is longer and each item in that chain more tedious. You need to make a crafting table, but instead of 4 planks like in vanilla, you need two logs and two flint. I'm gonna tell you now, you want to get at least 18 gravel to make 6 flint. You'll understand why in the next paragraph. For now, make your crafting table and gather some extra wood for your first set of wooden tools, requiring 9 planks and 8 sticks total. These tools are one of the only recipes to be exactly the same as vanilla, but as you've seen, the process to getting there is longer. Next you want to make yourself a chest for all the stuff you're about to go collect. Some of these items will be food, so make yourself a sword too. For all of this, you can follow the quest book instead of listening to me, but later on I promise you will appreciate me being descriptive about what to do.

Now, it's time to handle food. In GTNH you will burn hunger a lot faster than in vanilla and there are adverse effects for being too hungry. These effects include slowness, mining fatigue, and weakness with varying levels of intensity for every half hunch of hunger you are below three hunches. You will also start taking damage when you have no hunger bars at all. Basically, once you can't sprint, you absolutely must eat something. Unfortunately, you cannot eat the same food item too often. GTNH will track the last twenty food items eaten. If five or more of those items are the same, you will receive less hunger by consuming that item. Since this can be brutal initially, you get a 10 food item grace period. The game still tracks the foods you've eaten, but doesn't limit you in how much of an item you can consume during this time. Use this to eat as much of the best food you can find before you need variety in your life. With your mighty tools, collect some cobblestone and craft a furnace with the extra flint you should've crafted earlier. Use the remaining daytime to gather what wood and food items you can. The wood will get used to cook SOME meat items if you collect any, as well as make more tools for an overnight mining session in your hole. Another good food option is to collect plant gardens from the Biomes o Plenty mod. If you right click them, you will gather the gardens themselves, conserving inventory space now, and letting you break them later for food items.

Got all that? Good, because now it's very likely night time and you're about to die. Get into your hole, cook at most 5 of any meat products you have, and get to mining cobblestone. You need to collect 64 for one of the quests anyway, plus you need to increase the space because this is your home for the first week or more. Always remember to block off your entrance during the night so a rogue creeper doesn't wander in and really mess up your day.

Your next major task is getting a bed so that you don't have to wait out the night. If you're playing on hardcore mode, then you don't care about respawning, but for normal people, you will see this bed many times after death. First, you need a soft mallet, which will be used in the bed recipe, but also can be used to make a LOT of food variants. If you were lucky enough to find 6 of the same colored sheep and happen to have enough wood, this will be very easy. If not, you'll need to wait till morning. Either way, craft up your carpets and fence posts for the bed and now you can finally rest.

Aside from some "required" quests that don't actually inhibit your progress, you have officially completed the first quest tier and should have unlocked the Stone Age. Congratulations! It will only get harder from here! Always remember to accept your quest rewards as they give you a lot of useful resources that you either can't craft yet or can but the reward saves you time and effort. You should also do some of the side quests, either for the rewards or because you actually need the items they ask you to collect.
